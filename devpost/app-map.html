Catch the AI 🔍 app map

A one-page guide to where things live in this project, so you know where to start when you want to change something.

What the app is

You paste notes. The AI writes a short summary, then hides 3 mistakes in it. You catch each one by typing the correct idea in your own words, and a Review screen shows what to revise. There are only three main pieces:

The webpageindex.html style.css app.js
The four screens and all the game rules: counter, hints, stamps, Review.
The serverless functionapi/ai.js
The only place that knows the Groq key. It talks to the AI and double-checks what comes back.
The AI instructionsapi/_prompts.js
The rules the AI must follow and the JSON "forms" it must fill in.

Nothing is stored: there's no database, and a case lives only in the open page.

One action, followed through the code: "Check the Fix"

  1. You type your correction and press Check the Fix.
    On the Catch screen
  2. checkFix in app.js packs your answer with the correct fact, the wrong sentence and, if it's on screen, the 💡 hint question, then sends it to /api/ai.
  3. handler in api/ai.js (the "check-fix" part) adds the judging rules from CHECK_FIX_INSTRUCTIONS and asks Groq through askGroq.
  4. Groq (openai/gpt-oss-120b) fills in the form: {"verdict": "correct" | "close" | "wrong"}.
    External AI service
  5. Back in app.js: “correct” runs markCaught (red CAUGHT ✓ stamp, counter goes up, and confetti then showReview on the 3rd). “close” or “wrong” shows a message and, after 2 tries, the 💡 hint.

The route, stop by stop

A reference route: open these in your editor and use your editor's search (Ctrl+F) with the name shown.

1

app.js → search async function checkFix

Where your answer leaves the page. The hintQuestion line is the fix for your "rejecting" bug: once the hint is showing, the AI gets to see the question you're answering.

2

api/ai.js → search body.action === "check-fix", then api/_prompts.js → CHECK_FIX_INSTRUCTIONS

The function builds the question for the AI and attaches the rules. Those rules decide how strict the judging is ("judge meaning, not wording"). If something goes wrong here, the page gets a friendly {"error": "server"}, never an error code.

3

app.js → search function markCaught, then function showReview

What you see: the stamp, the counter and confetti. showReview then builds the Review screen from the round's numbers, using resultLabel and reviseList, with no AI involved.

A practice to reuse

Test AI instructions against answers you already know, and fix one rule at a time.

In this project, check-fix was tested with fixed answers whose right verdict was decided in advance: "demand goes down" → correct, "demand stays the same" → wrong, "price and demand are related" → close. Make-case was run several times on the sample notes and real B.Com notes, and each unfair mistake became one new rule (one fact per mistake, wrong in real life, never contradicting itself, different ideas, natural wording). The evidence for each change is recorded under devpost/checklist.md → Revisions.

Two lessons came out of that testing:

  • The AI copies the examples you give it. When a rule's "Bad" example used a real phrase from the notes ("per hour"), the AI started writing that phrase. The examples now come from unrelated subjects: fruit, boiling water and photosynthesis.
  • Instructions make good behavior likely, not guaranteed. That's why findCaseProblem in code still checks the shape of every case (exactly 3 mistakes, in different points, no stray symbols) and asks again once.

Next project: before writing a prompt, write 3–5 example inputs with the output you expect, run them after every change, and back up the rules that matter most with a check in code.
